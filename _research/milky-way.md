# The Fermi Bubbles

Towering ~25,000 light-years above and below the Galactic center are the **Fermi Bubbles** —
two enormous lobes of hot gas and cosmic rays, first seen in γ-rays, tracing a violent episode
of energy injection from the heart of the Milky Way. I probe them using **distant quasars and
AGN as backlights**, measuring ultraviolet absorption from cool gas clouds along the sight line.

- In the [first UV-absorption study of the *southern* bubble](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.10617)
  (*ApJ*, 2018) — first author — I mapped the kinematics and chemistry of these high-velocity
  clouds, showing the nuclear wind is actively entraining cool gas.
- A follow-up in [*Nature Astronomy*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41550-022-01720-0)
  (2022) found the clouds span **diverse metallicities**, pointing to *dual origins* — some gas
  lifted out of the disk, some already resident in the halo.

Together these help quantify how activity at the Galactic center recycles gas through the halo
— a process central to how galaxies like ours evolve.

# Earlier research

Before cosmology, my first projects (as an undergraduate researcher) were in Galactic and
stellar astrophysics:

- **How far is the Sun above the Galactic plane?** A purely geometric measurement of the Sun's
  height above the Milky Way's mid-plane, from the sky positions of stars —
  [Karim & Mamajek (2017), *MNRAS*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.08125).
- **How young stars spin up.** Measuring the rotation-period distributions of 4–10 Myr T-Tauri
  stars in Orion OB1 to constrain pre-main-sequence angular-momentum evolution —
  [Karim et al. (2016), *AJ*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.04333), my first first-author paper.
